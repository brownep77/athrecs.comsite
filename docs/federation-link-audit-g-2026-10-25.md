# Federation link audit G — 25 October 2026

Reviewed all 108 triathlon organisations whose primary link still used a World Triathlon membership profile after audit F. Every membership page was opened through structured retrieval and direct HTML inspection. Of these, 65 expose a Visit website link: 58 national or reference-site destinations and seven social/media destinations. The remaining 43 pages have no such link; this does not establish that no national website exists elsewhere.

## Changes

- Promoted 30 corroborated national websites while retaining their original membership references: Namibia, Rwanda, Zimbabwe, Bangladesh, Iran, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Uzbekistan, Virgin Islands, Cayman Islands, Cook Islands, Northern Mariana Islands, Tahiti, Azerbaijan, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Belarus, Faroe Islands, Gibraltar, Liechtenstein, Moldova, North Macedonia, Montenegro, Russia, Ukraine, Nigeria, Qatar, Kosovo and Aruba.
- Added a public qualification for Andorra while retaining its World Triathlon primary link. No unrelated athlete-route destination was added to the source registry or public directory links.
- Added 82 disabled manual sources and reviewed 108 existing membership-source rows. The registry now has 1,246 sources: 36 enabled and 1,210 disabled. All 740 directory organisations remain registered and all original source notes and references are retained.
- Assessed 49 deeper pages/documents; 48 were registered as qualified research references and one unsuitable athlete route was excluded. Registration does not activate collection or verify an athlete or result.

## Useful data sources and limits

Zimbabwe has 2026–27 calendar and results-document indexes and a 2025–26 results archive. One linked 11 October 2026 PDF was downloaded and its first page inspected. Its clock time, finish time and actual time columns are distinct; overall and category positions are also distinct, with explicit DNS/DNF statuses. Publisher: Zimbabwe Triathlon. A separate timing provider and chip/gun method were not established. No individual rows were matched, and no nationality column was present on the inspected page.

Kazakhstan's event search returned no matches, but its results archive exposes 2022–24 editions and its team page lists athletes. Uzbekistan has a 2026 event archive and country-labelled national-team cards; these supply source-reported representation, not independent citizenship or identity verification. Saudi Arabia, Russia and Ukraine also expose named athlete/team material. Ukraine's announcement concerns candidate athletes, not final selections. Iran's athlete list remains a loading placeholder, and Iranian-calendar years were not converted.

Aruba's homepage, About page and results history were recovered through a second retrieval method after the initial fetch was blocked. The results history includes 2026 and older editions, separate overall/category links and cancellation/postponement labels. Its current calendar is an image whose contents were not inspected. Historical malformed date text remains a warning, not an imported date.

Bosnia and Herzegovina's event table distinguishes organiser, championship level and discipline; multiple rows can describe the same meeting. Montenegro includes month-only and month-range dates. North Macedonia's calendar has repeated entries and a triathlon/duathlon label discrepancy. Faroe Islands' SST pages mix cycling and triathlon, social events and camps; selection standards are not athlete performances. Their generic Webscorer attribution must not override edition links pointing to RACE RESULT.

Cayman's calendar has dated November 2026 events and historical editions, including a 2024-titled race dated January 2025. Cook Islands lists a multi-day 2027 festival window, not one race start. Virgin Islands explicitly marks a cancellation and June schedule revisions. Ukraine's generic 08:00–15:00 windows are not verified starts. Oman labels its 2024 series as upcoming. Tahiti's tables include provisional points rankings and camps alongside competition documents. Qatar's year planner and several other calendar/results routes returned headings or navigation without rows.

## Held destinations

The 58 external destinations yielded 41 initial responses and 17 failures. A second retrieval method recovered Aruba; 16 remained unavailable. Thirty destinations supported primary-link upgrades, four remain qualified research references, and eight returned unsuitable, mismatched or incomplete content. A retrieval failure is a review limitation, not proof of an incorrect domain.

Chinese Taipei works over HTTP; HTTPS failed through two methods, so its public HTTPS membership link remains. Nepal and Fiji lead to Olympic-committee membership profiles. Andorra's homepage and calendar/results sections show federation content, but its linked athlete route redirects to unrelated gambling content, confirmed by fresh structured and direct HTML retrievals. An older search capture shows federation content. The conflict remains held; no claim about the cause or ownership is made.

UAE redirects to a general timing article, Ghana's domain carries unrelated content and disclaims representing the former federation, Uruguay identifies a differently named amateur organisation, Armenia shows a hosting placeholder, Colombia shows maintenance, the Philippines and Tunisia show server listings, and Mongolia's government-hosted section remains loading. Public membership references are retained for all of these.

No fixtures, start times, athlete facts, nationalities or individual results were imported, corrected or marked verified. Future fixture imports require edition-specific programme and start-time checks. Raw captures, PDF samples and original values remain in the private audit; public reports contain organisation/source qualifications only.

## Validation

Source-registry, organisation-coverage, bulk-source and sports-directory checks passed. Type, focused lint and development-build checks passed.
