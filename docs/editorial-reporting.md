# AthRecs results reporting

## Coverage

Publish original, useful reporting across AthRecs' supported sports. Priority
countries are the United Kingdom, Ireland, United States, New Zealand, Australia
and Canada. Other countries remain eligible when their competitions or athletes
provide a worthwhile story and reliable sources are available.

Use the World, Continent, National and Local views to help readers find coverage.
An article can appear in several relevant views while retaining one canonical
URL. Select World deliberately for international significance; an overseas venue
alone does not make a story worldwide news. Continental feeds group relevant
stories geographically and do not imply continental championship status.

Record each location as a complete country/area/county tuple. Use states,
provinces, regions and local administrative areas as appropriate. UK filtering
includes England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland; country geography never
establishes an athlete's nationality. Tag only the sports and places actually
covered. Do not duplicate an article for each geographic feed or fill empty
country pages with invented reports.

## Writing and length

Write substantial results reports of 500 words or more when verified results,
athlete background and event context support that length. Short announcements
and corrections can be shorter. Do not pad copy to reach a word count or promise
search rankings because an article is long.

Lead with the result and its human significance. Explain the race's key outcomes,
relevant podiums and categories, national or local interest, and source-supported
athlete progress. Keep overall, national, gender, age-group and wheelchair
classifications distinct. Credit participants and clubs accurately and avoid
invented quotations, on-course observations, records or personal bests.

Use a specific headline, a useful standfirst, short paragraphs, descriptive
subheadings and readable results tables. Add a unique SEO title and description,
an accurate date and byline, and relevant internal event and athlete links. Keep
keywords natural. The article template supplies canonical metadata, structured
article data, breadcrumbs, reading time and accessible headings/table controls.

Editorial guidance: [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)
and [Article structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article).
These are presentation and content-quality practices, not ranking guarantees.

## Results and sources

Follow `AGENTS.md` and the result-evidence policy. Open the organiser, governing
body or authorised results provider and inspect the relevant edition and rows.
Retain source precision and timing labels; preserve provisional or unofficial
qualifications. Entry totals are not finisher totals. A fundraising target is
not the amount raised. Attribute reported personal bests and distinguish them
from independently verified profile records.

Link and credit the original results provider in editorial coverage. Register
new research sources without enabling automatic collection. An editorial report
does not import results into athlete profiles or upgrade result verification.
Use only images with appropriate permission; an article can publish without one.

## Athlete profiles and follow-up

Every new article must declare all named athletes in `athleteNames`, including
people mentioned only in background prose. The queue also recognises existing
reviewed links and athlete-name columns in results tables. Do not assume prose
name extraction is complete.

Use **Admin → Athlete tools → Article athletes** for the staff review. It checks
the current directory, including private and claimed records, and distinguishes:

- Reviewed links to available public profiles.
- Possible existing profiles needing identity checks.
- No matching name, requiring wider research before creating a profile.
- Previously linked profiles that are no longer publicly available.

An exact or unique name match is a candidate, not an identity decision. Compare
the source and profile, using clubs, event history and other reliable evidence.
Check spelling variants, former names and the wider staff directory before
deciding that a profile is missing. A verified alias can be connected using its
existing public profile URL or slug.

Saving a link requires a staff identity confirmation, source URL and review note.
The review is recorded privately and can be removed. Public pages receive only
the approved name-to-slug mappings, checked against current profile visibility
and owner sharing preferences. Staff evidence and private candidates stay out of
public payloads. An unavailable live directory must report that limitation, not
mark athletes missing. Preview deployments cannot save link decisions.

Create genuinely missing profiles through the existing staff profile workflow
after research, then return to the article review to connect them. Do not create
duplicates, merge people automatically, change privacy, or send invitations as a
side effect of writing an article.

## Publication check

Check factual claims, original wording, complete athlete rosters, geography,
results qualifications, source credits, canonical metadata and mobile tables.
Run the editorial browse, athlete-review and report verifiers plus the repository
release checks. Publishing these features does not schedule recurring research
or unattended article publication.
